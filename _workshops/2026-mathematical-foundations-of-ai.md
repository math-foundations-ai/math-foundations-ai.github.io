<h2 id="overview">Overview</h2>

The workshop took place in Palmstedtsalen at Chalmers from 8 to 10 June 2026, and discussed how we can develop AI as a science with common theoretical foundations rather than only as an engineering field.

<h2 id="themes">Themes</h2>

<div class="topic-list">
  <article>
    <h3>Transformers</h3>
    <p>Transformers underpin large language models, but theoretical approaches to them remain fragmented. The workshop compared these approaches and looked for a common framework for analysing transformer architectures.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Diffusion models and flow matching</h3>
    <p>The theory of generative models is changing rapidly. Talks assessed current results in diffusion models and flow matching and identified open questions.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Associative memories</h3>
    <p>Associative-memory models have renewed relevance through their connections with learning and memorisation in transformers and diffusion models. The workshop linked classical results with recent work on these systems.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<section class="program-day" id="program-day-one" aria-labelledby="program-day-one-heading" data-program-day-panel hidden>
  <h3 id="program-day-one-heading"><span>8 June 2026</span>Diffusion models and associative memories</h3>
  <ol class="schedule-list">
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>8:30–9:15</time><strong>Welcome fika and registration</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>9:15–9:30</time><strong>Welcome address</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-luca-ambrogioni" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>9:30–10:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Luca Ambrogioni · Invited speaker</span><strong>How Out-of-Equilibrium Phase Transitions can Seed Pattern Formation in Trained Diffusion Models</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk described pattern formation in trained diffusion models as an out-of-equilibrium phase transition caused by instabilities in denoising dynamics. Its theoretical framework connects symmetries in the data and architectural constraints, including locality and translation equivariance, to collective spatial modes. Structure emerges when low-frequency modes become unstable and spatial correlations grow rapidly.</p>
          <p>The predictions were tested in a patch-based model, convolutional models trained on Fashion-MNIST and large ImageNet models. Across these settings, pattern formation coincided with increased correlation length and weakened low-frequency modes. Guidance applied near this critical stage improved class alignment compared with guidance applied at arbitrary times.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-chenxiao-ma" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>10:30–11:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Chenxiao Ma · Contributed talk</span><strong>The interplay between data structure and imbalance in the learning dynamics of diffusion models</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>This work studied how class heterogeneity and sampling imbalance affect the transition from generalisation to memorisation in diffusion models. A high-dimensional random-features model trained on Gaussian mixtures was analysed through its feature-covariance spectrum to obtain class-specific generalisation and memorisation times.</p>
          <p>Class variance was found to determine the learning order most strongly, with higher-variance classes learned first; centroid geometry had a secondary effect. Strong sampling imbalance could reverse this order and delay speciation for minority classes. Experiments with U-Nets on Fashion-MNIST supported the theoretical predictions.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>11:00–11:30</time><strong>Break</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-alberto-fachechi" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>11:30–12:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Alberto Fachechi · Invited speaker</span><strong>Dense L-Directional Hopfield Networks: Merging Modularity and Higher-Order Interactions for Pattern Disentanglement</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk began from the Hopfield model as a foundational associative-memory system and considered dense variants with higher-order interactions. It combined this dense-interaction setting with modular Hopfield networks whose modules interact anti-imitatively.</p>
          <p>The resulting dense, modular networks were analysed as a method for disentangling spurious nonlinear combinations of stored patterns, with applications to practical pattern-disentanglement problems.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>12:30–12:45</time><strong>Discussion</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>12:45–14:00</time><strong>Lunch</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-matteo-negri" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>14:00–15:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Matteo Negri · Invited speaker</span><strong>Detecting Creativity Geometrically: Hopfield networks as a Lens on Generative AI</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk used associative-memory energy landscapes to ask when generative AI generalises rather than memorises. Spurious mixture states, usually treated as defects, can create basins of attraction around unseen examples and therefore provide a geometric mechanism for generalisation.</p>
          <p>This viewpoint was extended to self-attention, interpreted as a pseudo-energy minimisation process with vector spins representing token embeddings, and to discrete diffusion language models. Attractors near test examples were proposed as a diagnostic for distinguishing memorisation from genuine generative behaviour.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-vincenzo-schimmenti" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>15:00–15:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Vincenzo Schimmenti · Contributed talk</span><strong>Equilibria of Modern Hopfield networks</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The work characterised equilibria in dense associative memories formulated as modern Hopfield networks. A convex-dual form of the energy maps the system to a phase-separation problem; minima correspond to pattern weights at fixed points of the retrieval dynamics, determined by the Gram matrix of embedded patterns.</p>
          <p>Bifurcations follow a hierarchy related to spectral clustering in the correlation matrix. The method reproduces replica-based results for Gaussian patterns and was tested on hierarchical toy models, gene-expression networks and standard machine-learning datasets.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>15:30–16:00</time><strong>Afternoon fika</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-beatrice-achilli" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>16:00–17:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Beatrice Achilli · Invited speaker</span><strong>Statistical Physics of Generative Diffusion</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The first part developed a general theory of speciation transitions in reverse diffusion. These sharp changes mark the point at which trajectories commit to data classes. A Bayesian description of class structure and free-entropy differences gives transition times for arbitrary target distributions, including classes that differ only in higher-order moments, and allows successive transitions at increasingly fine class resolutions.</p>
          <p>The second part examined memorisation when data lie on latent manifolds. Treating the empirical score as a proxy for the true score, the high-dimensional process was characterised with spin-glass methods. The analysis showed how structure in the data can reduce the usual dimensionality burden.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-enrico-ventura" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>17:00–17:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Enrico Ventura · Contributed talk</span><strong>Emergence of Distortions in High-Dimensional Guided Diffusion Models</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>This talk analysed how classifier-free guidance can reduce sample diversity by making its sampling distribution differ from the true conditional distribution. In high-dimensional Gaussian mixtures, dynamic mean-field theory predicts distortions when the number of classes grows exponentially with dimension; they vanish in the sub-exponential regime through a dynamical phase transition. In the infinite-class limit, distortions remain.</p>
          <p>Standard guidance schedules could not prevent variance shrinkage. A schedule with a negative-guidance window improved class separation and diversity in latent diffusion experiments. <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00716" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Related preprint ↗</a></p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>17:30–17:45</time><strong>Discussion</strong></li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section class="program-day" id="program-day-two" aria-labelledby="program-day-two-heading" data-program-day-panel hidden>
  <h3 id="program-day-two-heading"><span>9 June 2026</span>Geometric deep learning and transformers</h3>
  <ol class="schedule-list">
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>9:00–10:00</time><strong>Morning fika</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-daniel-persson" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>10:00–11:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Daniel Persson · Invited speaker</span><strong>Geometric deep learning: from equivariance to weather predictions</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk introduced geometric deep learning through invariance and equivariance, starting with convolutional neural networks and extending the discussion to manifolds, homogeneous spaces and gauge-equivariant models.</p>
          <p>Applications included spherical signals, computer vision, weather prediction and topological materials, showing how ideas from mathematics and physics can inform neural-network design.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>11:00–11:30</time><strong>Break</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-giovanni-marchetti" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>11:30–12:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Giovanni Luca Marchetti · Invited speaker</span><strong>Algebraic Geometry of Deep Learning</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>Neural networks parameterise spaces of functions, sometimes called neuromanifolds, whose geometry is connected to expressivity, sample complexity and implicit bias. For algebraic models, such as networks with polynomial activations, these spaces are semi-algebraic varieties.</p>
          <p>The talk presented this algebraic-geometric view and then focused on linear self-attention, an algebraic model of degree three. It covered the geometry of linear self-attention layers and parameter symmetries in deep attention networks as part of the emerging subject of neuroalgebraic geometry.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>12:30–12:45</time><strong>Discussion</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>12:45–14:00</time><strong>Lunch</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-freya-behrens" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>14:00–15:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Freya Behrens · Invited speaker</span><strong>Model Systems for attention, structure and memory in Transformers</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk isolated interacting components of language models in controlled settings. It examined how dot-product attention can move from positional to semantic behaviour as more data become available, and used counting tasks to compare architecture-dependent routes to parameter and compute efficiency.</p>
          <p>It also studied information storage in multilayer perceptrons and associative memories, including how correlations with unrelated inputs can support class inference. Related papers: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03902" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2402.03902</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11542" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2407.11542</a> and <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14457" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2506.14457</a>.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-oskar-allerbo" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>15:00–15:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Oskar Allerbo · Contributed talk</span><strong>Is supervised learning really that different from unsupervised?</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The work decomposed supervised learning into two stages: model parameters are selected without target values, after which the targets are incorporated without changing those parameters. A new model-selection criterion makes this possible without access to the targets.</p>
          <p>For linear ridge regression, the method's asymptotic out-of-sample risk was bounded relative to the optimum. Experiments covered linear and kernel ridge regression, smoothing splines, nearest neighbours, random forests and neural networks, with target-free training performing similarly to standard training. <a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=x09RDZBfdc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Paper ↗</a></p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>15:30–16:00</time><strong>Afternoon fika</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-peter-sukenik" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>16:00–17:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Peter Súkeník · Invited speaker</span><strong>One token to attend them all? Sink vs. diagonal patterns as mechanisms for attention switch and oversmoothing prevention</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk compared attention sinks and diagonal patterns as switching and anti-oversmoothing mechanisms. It derived geometric conditions for representing sinks, including the required alignment between a sink embedding and other embeddings, and specified when dense attention smooths more than sparse attention.</p>
          <p>Sinks were related to a hard attention switch with zero output. Allowing tokens to communicate with themselves produces a softer alternative through diagonal patterns. A quantitative cost comparison explained why pretrained transformers may favour sinks and clarified when attention layers behave like multilayer perceptrons.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>17:30–17:45</time><strong>Discussion</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>19:30</time><strong>Speakers' dinner</strong></li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section class="program-day" id="program-day-three" aria-labelledby="program-day-three-heading" data-program-day-panel hidden>
  <h3 id="program-day-three-heading"><span>10 June 2026</span>Transformers</h3>
  <ol class="schedule-list">
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>8:30–9:30</time><strong>Morning fika</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-alessio-giorlandino" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>9:30–10:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Alessio Giorlandino · Invited speaker</span><strong>From Initialisation to Factual Recall: Two Studies on Transformers</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The first study connected signal propagation through transformer networks at initialisation with trainability. The second studied factual recall as an associative-memory problem, using a toy task to derive sharp thresholds for a simple neural network to memorise correct associations.</p>
          <p>Related papers: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24333" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">signal propagation and trainability</a> and <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10795" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">factual recall</a>.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-alessandro-zambon" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>10:30–11:00</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Alessandro Zambon · Contributed talk</span><strong>Sampling at intermediate temperatures is optimal for training large language models in protein structure prediction</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The work introduced pseudo-Langevin dynamics for efficient Boltzmann sampling of feed-forward neural networks on large datasets using controlled mini-batches. This avoids the repeated full-batch gradients required by exact methods such as hybrid Monte Carlo.</p>
          <p>The method was used to sample transformer loss landscapes for protein-sequence models across temperatures. Unlike feed-forward networks, transformers showed no first-order-like transition and instead had a range of intermediate temperatures with favourable learning properties. Layer parameters were strongly conserved there when the embedding dimension was well chosen, while attention matrices predicted protein contact maps better at higher temperatures and larger embedding dimensions.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--pause"><time>11:00–11:30</time><strong>Break</strong></li>
    <li class="schedule-entry schedule-entry--talk">
      <details class="program-talk" id="talk-yedi-zhang" data-program-talk>
        <summary><time>11:30–12:30</time><span class="program-talk-heading"><span class="program-speaker">Yedi Zhang · Invited speaker</span><strong>Training Dynamics of In-Context Learning in Linear Attention</strong></span><i aria-hidden="true">＋</i></summary>
        <div class="program-abstract">
          <p>The talk analysed gradient-descent dynamics in multi-head linear self-attention trained for in-context linear regression. The dynamics contain exponentially many fixed points and move from saddle to saddle; this behaviour can be reduced to scalar ordinary differential equations.</p>
          <p>During training, the model performs principal-component regression in context, with the number of active principal components increasing over time. The results give a theoretical account of how in-context learning develops during gradient descent.</p>
        </div>
      </details>
    </li>
